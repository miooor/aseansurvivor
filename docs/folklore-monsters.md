# Malaysian folklore encounters

Each playable route stop has three folklore-inspired enemies. They arrive near the player at 24, 36 and 53 seconds of active campaign play. The third debut comes after the 45-second elite and its banner. The variants join ordinary waves six seconds after their respective debuts, at 30, 42 and 59 seconds. Each receives one third of the shared 0.5 wave weight. The roster covers eight states and the Federal Territory of Kuala Lumpur. The stage associations describe the campaign's adaptations. Stories, motifs and beliefs travel across places and can have several tellings.

The third set places invented creatures, shadows and objects in the campaign. It keeps devotional practices, places of worship, and sacred and historical figures outside the attacks. See [Respectful portrayal](../DESIGN.md#respectful-portrayal).

On the road to Johor's Palm groves, an **orang bunian wayfarer** gives directions back to the village clearing once per run. The wayfarer has no combat or loot role. The portrayal draws on Malay accounts of unseen people with dwellings and social lives and treats those accounts as beliefs with different interpretations. The character, dialogue and Johor placement are fictional. [Federal Territories Mufti's Office, including the Kamus Dewan definition and several interpretations](https://www.muftiwp.gov.my/ms/artikel/al-kafi-li-al-fatawi/1406-al-kafi-li-al-fatawi-siri-ke-479-apakah-itu-bunian?templateStyle=16)

## Combat and verification

The original regional signatures still serve as cache guardians and timed elites. Folklore attacks fix their marks at the start of the warning and deal skill damage on impact. Delayed marks resolve on the simulation clock. Defeating the caster clears unspent marks, and reduced motion retains the warnings. The wayfarer is run-local and absent from practice and the raid.

Run `/tests/stage-worlds.html` to check all three arrivals and wave gates per stage, all 27 sprite references, aimed and delayed attacks, cancellation, pause, keyboard and touch movement, and the wayfarer encounter. The third set uses its own 3×3 atlas. Polygon warnings share a convex outline with the hit test, including overlap with the player’s hit radius. See [wave three verification](../test-results/folklore-monsters-wave-3/) and [wave two verification](../test-results/folklore-monsters-wave-2/) for reports and combat screenshots.
